Qaf Institute is the learning arm of the Islamic Center of East Brunswick — a community built on the letter ق, the sacred root beneath the pen (qalam), the heart (qalb), the word (qawl), and the tribe (qabeelah).

Our mission

The Islamic Center of East Brunswick proudly presents Qaf: the programming and educational arm of our community.

Founded by Imam Safwan Eid, Qaf grows from a clear reading of our moment. A community that institutionalizes niyyah, intentionality. A center that teaches us, forms us, and gathers us around a cohesive worldview, a confident and thoughtful faith, and a refined engagement with the culture we live in. Qaf is that work made real, one living ecosystem of learning, worship, and fellowship, rooted in adab.

Every program shares a single letter, Qāf, and a single purpose: to help you know your faith, love Allah and His Beloved Prophet ﷺ, and belong to something real. Wherever you are on your journey, there is a seat here with your name on it.

Fall term: September 13 to December 20. Come as you are.

The Seven Stations

A single letter, read as an ascent

Each station names a qāf. Received at the base, the Word is grasped again at the summit — the same climb the mark already draws.

i · Qurʾān — Talaqqī

  1. 1

    Qurʾān

    قُرآن

    The recited Word received — the muṣḥaf at the base of the diamond. Everything above is a reading of what enters here.

    اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ

    “Recite in the name of your Lord who created.”

    Qurʾān, Sūrat al-ʿAlaq 96:1 — the first word to descend.
    Maqām
    Talaqqī — Reception. The posture of receiving a Word one did not author — listening before reasoning.
    Adab
    Presence and ṭahāra before the muṣḥaf. One recites before one interprets; the text is host, the student guest.

  2. 2

    Qalb

    قَلب

    The vessel the Word lands in — the ontological centre of the mark. Adab before the sciences, even in the geometry.

    نَزَلَ بِهِ الرُّوحُ الْأَمِينُ · عَلَىٰ قَلْبِكَ لِتَكُونَ مِنَ الْمُنذِرِينَ

    “The Trustworthy Spirit brought it down upon your heart, that you might be among the warners.”

    Qurʾān, Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:193–194 — revelation given an organ.
    Maqām
    Ḥuḍūr al-qalb — Presence of heart. The vessel made receptive, its veils thinned so the Word can settle.
    Adab
    Ikhlāṣ and taṣfiya — purifying intention so that knowledge lands in a heart cleared to hold it.
    • The Heart of the Qurʾānsun · After ʿAṣr
    • Loving the Prophet ﷺsun · After ʿIshāʾ
    • Chai Shaithu · 7:30 PM
    • Qalb Communityfri · 7:30 PM
  3. 3

    Qabas

    قَبَس

    The ember kindled in that heart — Mūsā moving toward the fire. The one warm light at the centre of the mark.

    إِنِّي آنَسْتُ نَارًا لَّعَلِّي آتِيكُم مِّنْهَا بِقَبَسٍ

    “I have perceived a fire; perhaps I can bring you a torch from it.”

    Qurʾān, Sūrat Ṭā Hā 20:10 — he sought a torch and found guidance.
    Maqām
    Ṭalab wa-himma — The seeking. The soul in motion toward light — moving to the fire rather than summoning it.
    Adab
    The humility of the ṭālib. What Mūsā found at the fire was hudā, not the ember he set out for — the seeker receives more than he asked.
    • The Light Withinwed · 7:30 PM
  4. 4

    Qiblah

    قِبلة

    The turn — fourth of seven, the hinge of the climb. The single act of the will between what is received and what is yielded.

    فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ

    “So turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque.”

    Qurʾān, Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:144 — the whole face, one direction.
    Maqām
    Tawajjuh — Orientation. Once one sees by the light, the whole self is turned to a single point.
    Adab
    Niyya and wholeness — the ordering of a scattered self around one qiblah, so that learning has a destination and not only a source.
    • The Fiqh of Worshipwed · After ʿIshāʾ
  5. 5

    Qanāʿah

    قَناعة

    The self made content — the nafs set in right relation to its provision, so knowledge is sought for its Giver, not the ego.

    قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَنْ أَسْلَمَ، وَرُزِقَ كَفَافًا، وَقَنَّعَهُ اللَّهُ بِمَا آتَاهُ

    “Successful is the one who submits, is granted sufficiency, and whom Allah makes content with what He has given him.”

    Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (raḍiyaLlāhu ʿanhumā). Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, Kitāb al-Zakāh, no. 1054.
    Maqām
    Riḍā — Contentment. The nafs at rest with kafāf — sufficiency — its appetite quieted.
    Adab
    Adab with rizq. The verb qannaʿahu shares the q-n-ʿ root: qanāʿah is a station Allah bestows, not merely a discipline one achieves.
    • Foundations of Faithsun · After Maghrib
  6. 6

    Qalam

    قَلَم

    The pen — the light now writes and transmits. Knowing becomes gift; what was received begins to overflow.

    الَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِالْقَلَمِ · عَلَّمَ الْإِنسَانَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ

    “Who taught by the pen — taught man that which he knew not.”

    Qurʾān, Sūrat al-ʿAlaq 96:4–5 — the same sūrah as the base, now turned outward.
    Maqām
    Tablīgh — Transmission. The one who received now conveys; ʿilm passes from a filled vessel to an empty one.
    Adab
    The pen as amāna — a trust transmitted without distortion. The teacher owes the chain the fidelity he was shown.
    • Qalamsun · 11:00 AM
    • Word to the Wise · A ḥadīthtue · After ʿIshāʾ
    • Word to the Wise · A versefri · After ʿIshāʾ
  7. 7

    Quwā

    قُوى

    The faculties gathered, ordered, empowered — the summit. Each power finally in its right place: taʾdīb completed.

    يَا يَحْيَىٰ خُذِ الْكِتَابَ بِقُوَّةٍ

    “O Yaḥyā, take the Book with strength.”

    Qurʾān, Sūrat Maryam 19:12 — the Book received at the base, now grasped with power.
    Maqām
    Tamkīn — Empowerment. Realized strength — a life that holds the Book with the fullness of its powers.
    Adab
    The end returns to the beginning, transformed. Word received becomes Word grasped — adab, in the al-Attas idiom, is every faculty put in its right place.

  • Dr. Ayesha Muzaffar

    The Loving Qurʾān · Reading, Recital & Ḥifẓ

    Dr. Ayesha Muzaffar is a dedicated Qurʾān and Hifz student for over 10 years. She completed Hifz-ul-Qurʾān with the Quran Literacy institute and is currently pursuing her ijazah. She is passionate about teaching the Qurʾān and helping students develop a meaningful connection with the Book of Allah through patience, encouragement, and individualized teaching.

    As a mother of two, she brings personal understanding and compassion to her work with children and families. Her dream is for everyone to find joy in learning and teaching the Qurʾān.

    Dr. Ayesha is a graduate of New York University, College of Dentistry. She has been in clinical practice for 14 years and is the founder and CEO of iCare4Smiles. She believes that knowledge is an amanah from Allah and strives to use her experience to serve and benefit her community.

  • Ustadh Gadeen Desouky

    Foundations of Faith & The Fiqh of Worship

    Gadeen Desouky has been studying the Islamic sciences for the past eight years, completing seven years at Al-Hamid Foundation, where he studied classical texts in Fiqh, Usūl al-Fiqh, Hadith Nomenclature, Tafsīr, Arabic Grammar and Rhetoric, and Islamic Beliefs.

    He graduated from Rutgers University, where he founded the Al-Ghazālī Club with the aim of bringing qualified scholars to the New Brunswick campus to teach Islamic sciences to college students.

    In 2024, he co-founded MarkTrack, a school management system serving Islamic institutes across the country.

    Currently, Gadeen is furthering his studies in Islamic theology through the Kalam 3.0 Institute under the guidance of Shaykh Hamza Karamali.

    Gadeen is passionate about developing a community that nurtures a generation of strong faith, and is looking forward to working with the ICEB community to bring that vision to fruition.

    • Foundations of Faith

  • Ustadh Omar Abbasi

    Loving the Prophet ﷺ · Sīrah & Shamāʾil

    Omar Abbasi is a student of knowledge whose training spans Arabic and the various Islamic sciences. He began his studies with Dr. Shadee Elmasry and continues to take classes at Darul Fath, where he also teaches Arabic and ʿaqīdah.

    He has a Bachelors Degree in Computer Science from Rutgers University and is currently completing another bachelor’s in the Islamic Sciences through Al-Azhar University. He studied the instrumental (tool) sciences, including naḥw, ṣarf, manṭiq, balāghah, and ādāb al-baḥth under scholars such as Dr. Muhammad Abdullah, Shaykh Ibrahim Al-Hameed and others. He is currently studying fiqh, uṣūl, ʿaqīdah, the sciences of ḥadīth, and other subjects with a number of teachers, and is also studying the Hanafi tradition under Shaykh Tabraze Azam.

    Alongside his studies and teaching, he has worked with Safina Society for several years and serves as Executive Producer of the NBF Podcast.

    • Loving the Prophet ﷺ

  • Shaheer Saud

    Qalb · The Qaf youth group

    Shaheer Saud is a Rutgers University graduate with a Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and minors in Data Science and Criminology. His professional background is in technology, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence, with experience across both engineering and leadership roles.

    Alongside his professional and academic pursuits, Shaheer is deeply involved in Islamic learning and community service. He is currently pursuing traditional Islamic studies through an ʿĀlim program and is passionate about continuing to grow in both Islamic knowledge and service to the Muslim community.

    Shaheer has been actively involved with the Islamic Center of East Brunswick (ICEB), particularly in youth programming, community events, and initiatives aimed at creating meaningful opportunities for young Muslims to connect with their faith and one another. He also serves as President of Future Leaders of Islam (FLOI) and has been involved in organizing educational, social, professional, and religious programs for Muslim youth and young adults.

    Through his work in technology, Islamic education, and community leadership, Shaheer hopes to use his skills to serve the Muslim community and help develop environments where the next generation of Muslims can grow in faith, character, knowledge, and leadership.
